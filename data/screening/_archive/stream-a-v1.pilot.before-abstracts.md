## 3. Machine learning-based carbon intensity estimation in smart grids with behind-the-meter distributed energy resources: A comprehensive review and future perspectives

`openalex:W7204641717` · 2026 · Next Energy · article

**Keywords:** Smart grid; Smart meter; Renewable energy; Distributed generation; Anomaly detection; Energy management; Resilience (materials science); Energy management system; Electricity; Computer science; Big data; Grid; Real-time computing; Electricity generation; Energy (signal processing); Engineering; Energy security; Data modeling; Electric power system; Greenhouse gas; Analytics; Cyber-physical system; Distributed computing; Energy engineering; Estimation

The rapid transition toward low-carbon energy systems has intensified the need for accurate carbon intensity (CI) estimation at high temporal resolutions. The CI estimation is intended to support carbon-aware energy management along with decarbonization strategies. Conventional approach based on average emission factors often fail to capture variations in electricity generation which are dynamic, particularly under increasing penetration of renewable energy resources (RERs) in energy mix. Recent advances in smart meter infrastructures and Behind-the-Meter (BTM) based distributed energy resources (DERs) viz., solar photovoltaic systems have created various prospects for improving the CI estimation. This review presents an all-inclusive assessment of recent advancements in spatio-temporal CI estimation using smart meter data, BTM based SPV disaggregation, machine learning (ML) based analytics and cybersecurity frameworks within in smart grids. This work critically examines preprocessing techniques, energy disaggregation methods, carbon emission flow models in line with robust forecasting approaches. Moreover, cybersecurity threats, mainly false data injection attacks (FDIAs) are discussed along with privacy preserving approaches viz., anomaly detection and encrypted mechanisms. This review further reveals that integrating CI estimation with cybersecurity measures enhances the overall resilience of carbon-aware smart grid applications. This integration also supports demand response programs (DRPs) by improvising the operational decision making in secured manner. This review also reveals the most promising ML approach among the discussed ones viz., hybrid models which are the combination of deep learning and ensemble model for precise forecasting in smart grid applications. Also, emerging artificial intelligence (AI) driven approaches are also conferred which are promising in estimating the CI aspect. Furthermore, this review identifies existing gaps and highlights emerging trends by emphasizing practical insights for researchers and policymakers to develop secure and data-driven smart grid infrastructure for sustainable energy transition to power a cleaner energy future.

## 4. A Privacy-Preserving-Framework-Based Blockchain and Deep Learning for Protecting Smart Power Networks

`ieee:8918446` · 2020 · IEEE Transactions on Industrial Informatics · Journals

**Keywords:** Anomaly detection; blockchain; cyber-physical system (CPS); deep learning; privacy preservation; proof of work (PoW); Data privacy; Machine learning; Power systems; Anomaly detection; Privacy

Modern power systems depend on cyber-physical systems to link physical devices and control technologies. A major concern in the implementation of smart power networks is to minimize the risk of data privacy violation (e.g., by adversaries using data poisoning and inference attacks). In this article, we propose a privacy-preserving framework to achieve both privacy and security in smart power networks. The framework includes two main modules: a two-level privacy module and an anomaly detection module. In the two-level privacy module, an enhanced-proof-of-work-technique-based blockchain is designed to verify data integrity and mitigate data poisoning attacks, and a variational autoencoder is simultaneously applied for transforming data into an encoded format for preventing inference attacks. In the anomaly detection module, a long short-term memory deep learning technique is used for training and validating the outputs of the two-level privacy module using two public datasets. The results highlight that the proposed framework can efficiently protect data of smart power networks and discover abnormal behaviors, in comparison to several state-of-the-art techniques.

## 5. Systematic Review of Big Data Applications for Sustainable Smart Campuses: Trends, Technologies, and Impact Metrics

**Keywords:** Big data; Smart campus; Artificial intelligence; Internet of things; Machine learning; Training; Resistance; Technological innovation; Ethics; Data privacy; Transforms; Big Data; Sustainable development; Artificial intelligence; Investment

This article explores how big data and artificial intelligence (AI) can be used on university campuses to facilitate sustainability. Through the analysis of these case studies from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), Nanyang Technological University (NTU) Singapore, and the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Madras, we extract successful applications that improve energy efficiency, lower greenhouse gas emissions, and create behavioral changes in students and staff. The results show that real-time data analytics and AI-driven predictive models enable universities to effectively determine resource demands and transition towards more sustainable practices. But problems like the complexity in data management, lack of skilled manpower, high capital investment & resistance are the hindrances in the implementation of these technologies. Furthermore, moral and ethical issues of data privacy and compliance with laws are also obstacles to the implementation. This study underscores the need for universities to pursue holistic solutions to these challenges, including promoting interdisciplinary collaborations and providing training opportunities to elevate the data literacy of the academy. Opportunities for future research include building on the development of Big Data capabilities, increasing engagement through personalized sustainability programs and developing benchmarks to assess the impact of sustainability activities. In summary, this research highlights the huge opportunities to transform universities by integrating big data and AI to meet sustainability goals and for commitment to continued investment and innovation to overcome current limitations so that the benefits of sustainable campus management can be reached.

## 6. Leveraging Blockchain and AI for Efficent Carbon Credit Tracking and Management

`ieee:11341143` · 2025 · 2025 IEEE 2nd International Conference on Information Technology, Electronics and Intelligent Communication Systems (ICITEICS) · Conferences

**Keywords:** Carbon Credit; Blockchain Technology; SmartContracts; Emission Reduction; Carbon Offset; Smart contracts; Carbon dioxide; Real-time systems; Blockchains; Recording; Security; Artificial intelligence; Information technology; Greenhouse gases; Carbon offsets

As climate change becomes an increasingly urgent global concern, the use of carbon credit systems has gained significant momentum. These systems aim to reduce greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions by allowing individuals, companies, and governments to compensate for their carbon output through the purchase of carbon credits-units that represent certified emission reductions. Despite their potential, traditional carbon credit frameworks face critical limitations, including inefficiency, fraud, limited transparency, and delays in verification. To overcome these barriers, this study introduces a novel framework that combines blockchain technology with carbon credit mechanisms. This integration aims to improve transparency, boost operational efficiency, and build trust in carbon offset processes. By utilizing blockchain's decentralized and immutable ledger, the system ensures secure, transparent, and tamper-resistant recording of emissions data. The use of smart contracts automates the issuance and trading of carbon credits, eliminating the need for third-party intermediaries and reducing associated costs. Furthermore, the framework introduces tokenized carbon credits, enabling real-time transactions and fractional ownership. This opens the market to a wider audience and increases accessibility. The system also incorporates artificial intelligence (AI) to generate tailored suggestions for users seeking to lower their carbon footprint based on their personal habits. By offering verified carbon credit purchases alongside AI-driven insights, users can make better-informed decisions for offsetting their emissions. The blockchain infrastructure guarantees the authenticity and security of all transactions, giving users assurance in the legitimacy of their climate action efforts.
