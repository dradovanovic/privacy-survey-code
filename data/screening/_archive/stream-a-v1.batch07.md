In smart grids, smart meters periodically collect users’ fine-grained multi-dimensional energy data, which poses great concerns on users’ privacy and security. Accurate household energy forecasting benefits from granular smart meter data, yet the EU General Data Protection Regulation demands data minimization. Existing privacypreserving approaches (differential privacy, federated learning) protect data after collection. We ask a complementary question: which feature categories can be removed entirely before collection? We propose a feature-level privacy analysis that organizes forecasting inputs into four privacy levels, from highly invasive appliance-level activity traces to publicly available metadata. Through evaluation across three household energy datasets (Canada, France, United Kingdom), representing basic metering, circuit-level monitoring, and fully instrumented smart homes, we quantify the forecasting cost of progressively removing private data. At the strictest privacy level, only datasets with building metadata (occupancy, house size) outperform a persistence baseline. Under the recommended collection policy, new household predictions stabilize within 6 hours of meter deployment. All findings replicate under Ridge regression, confirming model independence. These results provide empirical guidance for GDPR-compliant data minimization in smart meter infrastructure.

## 760. A Secure Federated Learning Framework for Residential Short-Term Load Forecasting

`ieee:10173657` · 2024 · IEEE Transactions on Smart Grid · Journals

**DOI:** 10.1109/tsg.2023.3292382

**Keywords:** Byzantine attack; federated learning; Internet of Things (IoT); load forecasting; smart grid; Load modeling; Load forecasting; Data models; Computational modeling; Predictive models; Servers; Training

Smart meter measurements, though critical for accurate demand forecasting, face several drawbacks including consumers’ privacy, data breach issues, to name a few. Recent literature has explored Federated Learning (FL) as a promising privacy-preserving machine learning alternative which enables collaborative learning of a model without exposing private raw data for short term load forecasting. Despite its virtue, standard FL is still vulnerable to an intractable cyber threat known as Byzantine attack carried out by faulty and/or malicious clients. Therefore, to improve the robustness of federated short-term load forecasting against Byzantine threats, we develop a state-of-the-art differentially private secured FL-based framework that ensures the privacy of the individual smart meter’s data while protect the security of FL models and architecture. Our proposed framework leverages the idea of gradient quantization through the Sign Stochastic Gradient Descent (SignSGD) algorithm, where the clients only transmit the ‘sign’ of the gradient to the control centre after local model training. As we highlight through our experiments involving benchmark neural networks with a set of Byzantine attack models, our proposed approach mitigates such threats quite effectively and thus outperforms conventional FedSGD models.
