This systematic literature review examines the application of artificial intelligence and machine learning technologies in strengthening cybersecurity frameworks for digital energy platforms. Adopting the PRISMA methodology, we analyzed 132 academic publications from 2010–24 across 72 sources, employing comprehensive bibliometric analysis and structural topic modeling to identify thematic patterns. The review revealed five distinct research domains: Critical infrastructure cybersecurity (33%), intrusion detection systems and anomaly detection (23%), machine learning and deep learning for threat detection (17%), IIoT and smart grid security (14%), and data-driven security system development (13%). Temporal analysis demonstrated an evolutionary shift from general infrastructure protection concepts toward specialized implementation approaches, with significant growth in publications (77.24% annually) and citations (25.92 per document). The field showed remarkable interdisciplinary collaboration (4.52 coauthors per document) but limited international cooperation (34.09%). Correlation analysis revealed that research streams typically develop in parallel rather than through integrated approaches, with the strongest potential integration opportunity between data-driven security systems and IoT-specific solutions. These findings advance theoretical understanding of research priority evolution in response to emerging technological paradigms and provide evidence-based guidance for industry stakeholders seeking integration opportunities. The review highlights the need for interdisciplinary frameworks incorporating regulatory, human factors, and economic perspectives alongside technical solutions, and empirical validation of AI-enabled security frameworks in operational energy environments to translate theoretical advances into practical security improvements for increasingly vulnerable critical infrastructure.

These systems generate heterogeneous data in bulk, which become susceptible to cyber-attacks, particularly the stealthy false data injection attacks (FDIAs). The existing centralized FDIA detection algorithms often breach data privacy and fail to perform effectively in highly dynamic and distributed environments, such as IoTES. To resolve the issue, initially, a recurrent deep deterministic policy gradient is utilized to invent an experience-driven FDIA in a complex IoTES. The attacker intends to intelligently exploit the data integrity of smart energy meters with insufficient knowledge of the system. The proposed approach is capable of parallel computing and can reliably identify the stealthy FDIA on all the nodes simultaneously. It is worth noting that the ETSI 103 104 specification describes an “Interoperability Test Specification for CoAP Binding of ETSI M2M Primitives”, which is of a particular importance in terms of interoperability with IETF standards.
